## Design Specificity Verdict

The design is coherent but insufficiently specific to Fisher. Alternating black and white canvases, Apple-like typography, blue links, and pill buttons create a polished gallery, but the visual identity is borrowed rather than built from quantitative research, trading infrastructure, experimentation, or Fisher's personality. An unrelated developer could reuse this design by replacing only the text and images.

## Overall Impression

The page starts calm and confident, peaks at the first project, then loses momentum across several oversized and structurally identical project tiles. The blog becomes a long archive rather than a guided reading experience, and the contact ending does little to convert interest into a meaningful next step. The biggest opportunity is to replace the Apple product-gallery metaphor with a portfolio language built around evidence: system diagrams, research artifacts, outcomes, constraints, and the thinking behind the work.

## What's Working

- The hero has strong contrast, clear hierarchy, and an obvious first action.
- Alternating project canvases give the featured work an editorial rhythm.
- The styling is restrained and internally consistent: one accent, limited chrome, and little decorative noise.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] The visual identity belongs more to Apple than to Fisher

**Why it matters:** Visitors may admire the surface without learning what makes Fisher distinctive as a quantitative developer and systems engineer.

**Fix:** Build the visual world from authentic artifacts: architecture diagrams, performance figures, research notebooks, terminal output, market-data structures, and concise role/outcome statements. Use those artifacts as composition, not decoration.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape homepage redesign`

### [P1] Project tiles are dramatically over-tall

**Why it matters:** Desktop tiles use roughly 760–820px minimum heights and mobile tiles remain around 610px even when content is short. Excessive scrolling reduces the chance visitors reach later work, writing, or contact.

**Fix:** Let content determine height, reserve the largest treatment for one flagship project, and use tighter editorial layouts for secondary projects.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout demo.html`

### [P1] Mobile hides key destinations

**Why it matters:** At 390px, Experience and Contact disappear from navigation, making the most valuable conversion paths harder to find for interrupted mobile visitors.

**Fix:** Keep all destinations in a compact menu, preserve one prominent contact action, and make the logo a Home link.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt demo.html`

### [P2] The portfolio shows technology but not enough proof

**Why it matters:** Descriptions and tags explain topics, but not Fisher's role, scale, constraints, measured outcomes, or engineering judgment. Recruiters and clients cannot quickly assess impact.

**Fix:** Give each featured project a consistent Role / Problem / Decision / Outcome layer and include concrete evidence where available.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable bolder demo.html`

### [P2] Hero and blog choices lack prioritization

**Why it matters:** “View My Experience” and “Full Resume” lead to the same page, while nine blog posts receive nearly equal treatment. Both areas ask visitors to choose without a meaningful hierarchy.

**Fix:** Give the hero one distinct primary action and one genuinely different secondary action. Feature a small number of essays and move the remainder into a clearly labeled archive.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill demo.html`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan — first-time visitor:** The duplicate hero actions create immediate uncertainty. Dense terms such as quantitative research and live trading infrastructure lack a plain-language explanation. On mobile, Experience and Contact are missing from navigation.

**Riley — deliberate evaluator:** Inline clickable blog headings and separate “Read more” links create inconsistent semantics. External links open new tabs without warning. The icon-only theme control does not clearly communicate its current state.

**Casey — distracted mobile visitor:** Long, fixed-height project cards demand extensive thumb scrolling. Important actions are concentrated at the top, the sticky navigation can crowd section headings, and multiple GIF assets may be costly on a slower connection.

## Minor Observations

- The logo is not a Home link.
- The Alchemist title is too long for a display heading; a shorter title plus subtitle would scan better.
- Technology tags look interactive even though they are not filters.
- Contact provides an address but no guidance about appropriate opportunities or what response to expect.
- Blog metadata is small and low-contrast.
- The contact lead uses an undocumented `1.2rem` font size.

## Questions to Consider

- What should a visitor remember about Fisher five minutes after leaving?
- Should the signature visual come from real research and trading-system artifacts rather than a borrowed product-gallery style?
- Is the primary goal employment, consulting work, collaboration, readership, or a deliberate combination?
- Which single project deserves to define the first impression?
